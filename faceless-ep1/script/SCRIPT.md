- So you started a faceless channel too. Sixty days in, and it looks like this. [your post card + flat analytics]
- Thirty-four videos. Three hundred and eleven views. Total.
- The algorithm isn't broken. The big channels do three things that most new faceless channels get wrong. [1 · 2 · 3]
- In this video, we'll look at what they do right, where you're probably going wrong, and then build a channel step by step, with the exact sites to open and what to click on each one. [roadmap]

## WHAT THEY DO RIGHT
- First, they own one idea. Kurzgesagt explains big science questions, with the same look in every video. Zack D. Films answers one strange question in under a minute. You know what you're getting before you click.
- Second, the picture and the voice are made for each other. Every sentence puts something new on screen.
- And third, nothing is copied. Every upload is a new idea, made on purpose.

## WHAT YOU'RE PROBABLY DOING WRONG
- Now compare that to the usual faceless setup. Pick a niche from a list of the most profitable niches. Ask AI for a script. Paste it into a voice tool. Cover it in stock footage. Upload. Then do it again thirty times.
- That is exactly what YouTube changed its rules to catch. In July 2025, YouTube renamed its repetitious content policy to inauthentic content, to make it clear that it covers videos that are mass-produced or repetitive. [policy card]
- Using AI is allowed. Uploading the same template over and over isn't.
- And if you're still hunting for ideas on YouTube's Trending page, it's gone. YouTube removed it in July 2025 and replaced it with Charts. [browser: youtube.com/feed/trending → REMOVED]
- So here's how to do it properly. Step by step. [particles: STEP BY STEP]

## STEP 1 — Find a niche with proof
- Step one is picking a niche. And the rule is simple: don't guess.
- Open YouTube and search for a topic you could make a hundred videos about. Then open the filters, and pick videos from this year. [browser: youtube.com search + Upload date filter]
- You're looking for one thing: a small channel with a video that blew up. A channel with eight thousand subscribers and a video with nine hundred thousand views tells you the topic is pulling people in, not the channel's name. [8.2K vs 900K card]
- Copy that channel's link into Social Blade. It shows how fast the channel is growing, and how often it posts. [browser: socialblade.com]
- Then check the topic on Google Trends. Change the search type from Web Search to YouTube Search, so you see what people look for on YouTube, not on Google. [browser: trends.google.com dropdown]
- If the line is flat or going up, and you found three small channels winning with it, you have your niche. If not, try the next topic.
- But a niche only tells you what to make. It doesn't tell you which videos people will actually click. That's step two.

## STEP 2 — Find what people click
- Open vidIQ and turn on its Outliers view. It lists videos that got many times more views than their channel normally gets. [browser: vidiq.com, outlier multipliers like 12x, 31x]
- Save the ten biggest outliers in your niche. Don't copy them. Look for the pattern in the titles: what each one promises the viewer.
- Write that pattern down as a sentence. For example: a mystery, a number, and a reason to doubt the official answer. [title-pattern card with colour-coded parts]
- Now you know what people click. Next you need a script that keeps them watching.

## STEP 3 — Write the script with Claude
- Open claude.ai and give it three things: your topic, your ten outlier titles, and one rule. The first fifteen seconds have to show the viewer something they didn't know. [browser: claude.ai, prompt typed in]
- Ask for the hook three different ways, and pick the one you'd click yourself.
- Then rewrite the hook in your own words. That rewrite is the human part YouTube's policy is looking for, and it's what makes your channel sound like you. [your edit, highlighted]
- A good script read in a flat voice still loses people. So the voice is next.

## STEP 4 — Give the channel a voice
- Record yourself on a phone in a quiet room, or use an AI voice like ElevenLabs. [browser: elevenlabs.io voice library]
- If you use AI, pick one voice and keep it for every video. Over time it becomes the sound of your channel.
- Read the script out loud once before you generate anything. Any sentence you stumble on, shorten.
- Now the voice needs something to look at.

## STEP 5 — Make the visuals match every sentence
- This is where faceless channels win or lose. The rule from the big channels: something new on screen every few seconds, and it shows what the voice is saying.
- Go through your script and write one visual next to each sentence. A number becomes a big number. A website becomes a browser window. A quote becomes a card. [script with a visual column]
- Build them in Canva or CapCut, or have Claude write motion graphics in code, like this video. [browser: canva.com, capcut.com]
- Then the last mile: getting people to click.

## STEP 6 — Upload so it gets tested properly
- In YouTube Studio, use Test & Compare to upload up to three thumbnails. YouTube shows each to different viewers and keeps the one that gets watched most. [browser: studio.youtube.com, thumbnail test panel]
- Write the title from your outlier pattern, not from your script.
- If any part of the video shows realistic people or events that were made with AI, turn on the altered or synthetic content setting.

## STEP 7 — Know the finish line
- To earn from ads, YouTube currently asks for a thousand subscribers, plus four thousand watch hours in a year or ten million Shorts views in ninety days. These numbers change, so always check YouTube's help center before you plan around them. [browser: support.google.com/youtube]
- Ten good videos that each teach one thing will get you further than a hundred copies.

## OUTRO
- Pick one niche. Make ten videos. Change one thing each time and watch what the numbers do.
- If this helped, subscribe. Next video, we build Step 5 live: a full motion graphics video made with Claude, from script to export. [subscribe card]
